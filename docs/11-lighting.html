Why the sun is fully realtime

Verified — and it settles the biggest open question in the project Mixed lighting with Shadowmask requires the light to be Mixed, the light to cast shadows, and the GameObject to be static. Our ~18,000 ground-cover instances are drawn with DrawMeshInstancedIndirect and have no GameObjects at all — so they can never be shadowmasked, under any configuration. The decision makes itself: one fully realtime directional sun for everything. Distance Shadowmask stays available for genuinely static authored geometry, but at one light and this scene composition it buys almost nothing.

This is a good outcome, not a compromise. A realtime sun means the six era moods cost nothing to implement, there is no bake to invalidate when the map generator changes, and the age-advance building swap — which replaces every building mesh in place — cannot corrupt lighting data that does not exist.

Shadow distance is set by the camera, not the map

URP stretches the shadow map across the URP Asset's Shadows → Max Distance. Shadow pixel density is therefore a function of that distance and nothing else — the 240/420/640 m map extents are irrelevant to it. Unity's own worked example: dropping Max Distance from 40 to 10 lets you drop the shadow map from 2048 to 1024 px and still get higher quality near the camera.

So: Max Distance tracks the camera's 18–90 m zoom band plus visible frustum depth at full zoom-out. Two caveats carried from the verification, both real:

  • That 40→10 / 2048→1024 arithmetic is quoted at Cascade Count 1 and does not transfer directly to the 2–4 cascade setup we will actually ship (Unity's default is 4 cascades over 150 m).
  • Changing Max Distance at runtime causes visible shadow popping. If we tie it to zoom, step it between a few discrete zoom bands and test whether the step is hidden — do not lerp it per-frame.

Bias is a bounded window, not a ramp

Unity 6 manual, verbatim and stable across 6000.0–6000.4:

"By adjusting the shadow bias values you can reduce or eliminate such shadow artifacts
 as shadow acne, shadow detachment (also known as peter-panning), light leaking,
 and self-shadowing."

"Using high shadow bias values might result in light leaking through meshes. This is
 where there is a visible gap between the shadow and its caster."

Start at Depth ~0.02–0.05, Normal ~0.5–1.5 and tune inside that window. Excess Depth bias pushes the shadow away from the caster; excess Normal bias narrows the caster and bleeds light at the contact point. They are different artifacts with different fixes.

Two things to know before you read Unity's docs on this The docs never mention terrain. This is generic surface guidance that happens to apply; there is no terrain-specific published figure.
Unity's vocabulary is muddy. What the manual calls "light leaking" (a gap between shadow and caster) is what the wider graphics literature calls peter-panning. Reason about the mechanism, not the label.

refuted 0-3

The belief that the sun's bias must be tuned on the Light component in Custom mode rather than the URP Asset default failed verification. Either location works. Set it wherever is convenient.

Indirect light — Adaptive Probe Volumes

APV is the right choice here. It auto-places probes (replacing manual Light Probe Groups), samples per-pixel rather than per-GameObject — which removes the seams you otherwise get between many small adjacent objects, exactly our case with props and ground cover — and streams data larger than memory.

The limit that matters APV is bake-only. It is not realtime GI. It gives us good, cheap, seam-free indirect light for a fixed lighting condition — it does not give us indirect light that follows a moving sun.

The six era moods — realtime, not Lighting Scenarios

Verified, and it saves a large amount of work APV offers exactly two runtime mechanisms for changing lighting: Lighting Scenarios and sky occlusion. Scenarios are the more accurate of the two — but sharing and blending scenarios requires identical Light Probe counts and positions across every bake, and age advancement swaps every building mesh in place, which breaks exactly that requirement.

Therefore: drive the six era moods with realtime sun colour, angle and intensity, plus fog and sky. No scenario baking, no blending constraints, no bake invalidated by an age advance.

refuted 0-3

The claim that sky occlusion needs only a single bake to support a full day-night cycle and outperforms scenario swapping failed verification. Do not plan a day/night cycle around it without testing it first.
