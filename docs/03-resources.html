03 · Economy

Resources & Minerals

Nine resources exist. Never more than six are on screen at once. Three are age-gated and simply do not appear in the UI until their age arrives, which is how a nine-resource economy stays readable.

Why uranium is rare on purpose Two or three deposits on the whole map means the Atomic Age is a place you have to go and hold, not a number you reach. It converts the endgame from an economy race into a territorial fight over three points. That is a better endgame and it costs nothing to implement — it is one line in the map generator.

A single Age I Rune Hall with two scholars makes 0.20 + 2×0.15 = 0.50 Knowledge/s. The 120 Knowledge for Feudal therefore takes 240 seconds of a fully staffed hall — four minutes in which two of your citizens are producing nothing edible. That is the intended pain.

The economy in one sanity check

A design is only real if the numbers close. Walking this through by hand once, now, is worth more than any amount of later tuning:

MINUTE 0    1 citizen. 200 food, 200 wood, 0 stone, 0 metal in the bank.
MINUTE 1    Citizen forages. 0.50 food/s × ~70% uptime (walking) = 0.35 food/s.
            Longhouse trains citizen #2: 50 food, 20 s.
MINUTE 4    4 citizens. ~1.4 food/s, first hut up (pop cap 5 → 10).
MINUTE 8    8 citizens. Split 4 food / 3 wood / 1 stone. Rune Hall goes up.
MINUTE 10   2 scholars in. Knowledge starts at 0.50/s. Economy now 6 working citizens.
MINUTE 14   120 Knowledge banked → FEUDAL AGE research begins, 60 s timer.
MINUTE 15   AGE II. Buildings swap. Gold appears in the UI for the first time.
This checks out Age II at ~15 minutes against a target of ~14. Close enough that the numbers above can be typed straight into the first prototype and tuned from play, rather than guessed again.

Units cost population: citizen 1, most infantry 1, cavalry 2, siege 3, tank 4, aircraft 5, ship 4–8. A 200 cap is therefore roughly 60 citizens plus a 30-unit modern army, which is the right scale for the map size in 08 · Map.

Rules that keep the economy honest

  • Everything finite is visibly finite. A tree that has been chopped looks chopped. A depleted ore outcrop leaves a pit. The player should never have to read a tooltip to know a resource is running out.
  • Drop-off distance is the real game. Gather rates are close to each other on purpose; the skill is in where you put the storehouse. This is the one piece of RTS economy that rewards attention every single minute.
  • No resource is strictly dominant. Gold converts at a deliberately bad rate (see below) so it is a smoother, not a substitute.
  • Markets convert at 1.4 : 1 against you, drifting worse the more you trade the same pair in a short window. Prevents a solved-economy exploit.
  • Upkeep exists from Age IV. Factories consume coal per second whether or not you are using them; aircraft consume oil while airborne. This is what makes Age IV's “supply collapse” loss condition real rather than flavour text.
To tune first, in this order 1. Age advance costs (sets the whole pace). 2. Citizen training cost and time (sets the opening). 3. Drop-off walk speed (sets how much micromanagement is rewarded). 4. Military costs (sets whether rushing beats teching). Everything else can wait.
