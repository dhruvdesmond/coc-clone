02 · Progression

The Six Ages

Rise of Nations has eight. We have six, because each age is a complete art set and six is what two people and a render farm can actually finish. Six is also enough for the arc to land: you can still feel the distance from a wooden spear to a warhead.

What each age actually changes

An age is not a stat bump. Each one introduces one new resource, one new movement domain or range band, and one new way to lose. If a proposed age cannot fill all three columns, it is not an age.

How you advance

Three gates, all three required. This is the Rise of Nations structure, which correctly makes advancement a plan rather than an accumulation.

GateWhat it meansDesign intent
1 · Knowledge costPay a Knowledge lump sum at the Library / University / LabYou must have invested in non-productive buildings much earlier
2 · Prerequisite techsResearch n techs across the four branches firstForces breadth; stops a pure-military rush from teching
3 · Research timerA real-time countdown while it researchesA visible window in which you are vulnerable — the opponent can see it and punish it

The four research branches

Every tech belongs to exactly one. Age advancement requires techs spread across them.

  • Civic — population cap, citizen speed, build speed, territory radius
  • Commerce — gather rates, caravan yield, storage, resource conversion
  • Military — unit damage, armour, new unit unlocks, formations
  • Science — Knowledge output, research speed, line of sight, and the age advance itself

Catch-up, and why it is not charity If an opponent has already reached an age, the cost drops 20% on Knowledge and 10% on everything else. Two or more ages ahead doubles it to 40% / 20%. This is straight from Rise of Nations and it is load-bearing: it keeps a losing player in the match without handing them free units, and it means the leader's advantage is time, not permanent separation. Implement it before the first balance pass, not after — it changes every other number.

The transition moment

Rule 4 in 01 · Concept says ages are total. Here is what that means in production terms, because it is expensive and it must be planned, not discovered:

LayerWhat happens on advanceImplementation
BuildingsEvery existing building swaps to its next-age model in placePrefab variant table keyed by (buildingId, age); cross-fade 0.8 s with a dust puff
UnitsExisting units do not upgrade. New ones come out modern.Deliberate: it makes the old army feel obsolete, which is the point
TerrainUnchanged. The land is the constant.Nothing to do — and that is the contrast that sells it
MusicStem swap on the bar lineSix stems, one key, same tempo family. See 09 · FX

Cost warning, stated honestly “Every building swaps model” means 6 variants × ~18 buildings = 108 building models if taken literally. We will not do that. The real plan: 4 core buildings get all 6 variants (Town Centre, House, Barracks, Wall) = 24 models; everything else exists in 2–3 ages only and is replaced rather than upgraded. That is ~70 building models total. Budgeted in 05 · Assets.

The story the ladder tells

There is no cutscene campaign at first. The progression itself carries the meaning, and it should be legible without a word of text:

  • I → II. You stop living on the land and start defending it. The first wall is the first admission that someone is coming.
  • II → III. The wall stops working. Everything you built in stone becomes decoration.
  • III → IV. Production leaves human hands. Your citizens stop making things and start feeding machines that make things.
  • IV → V. Distance dies. The front line is no longer a line.
  • V → VI. You acquire the ability to end the match instantly, and discover that so did they.

That last beat is the whole game's argument, and it is why the missile silo is worth the art. The player spends 70 minutes building a civilisation and is handed a button that makes all of it irrelevant.
