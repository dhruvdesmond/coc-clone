12 · The RoN core

Territory, Borders & Nations

This document exists because the brief is a Rise of Nations clone, and the first eleven documents had RoN's economy without RoN's map. Borders are the thing Rise of Nations is actually remembered for, and nothing else here substitutes for them.

What was missing, stated plainly Docs 01–11 already had the Knowledge economy, the four research branches, the three-gate age advance and the catch-up discount — all straight from RoN, all correct. But they described a game where you build a base and fight over resource nodes. That is not Rise of Nations. In RoN you fight over the map itself, and the map is coloured. Everything below is the correction.

1. Borders — strategy made visible as colour

Every city projects a territory radius. The union of those radii is your nation, drawn on the ground as a saturated colour wash with a hard edge at the frontier. You can see, at a glance and without reading a number, exactly who owns what and where the pressure is.

You can only build inside your own borders

This single rule is what makes territory matter rather than decorate. Expanding is not "walk somewhere and place a building" — it is found a city, wait for the border to bloom, then build. Which means expansion is visible to your opponent a full minute before it is useful to you, and it is attackable in that window.

2. Attrition — the reason invading is hard

The mechanic that makes borders load-bearing Enemy units inside your borders take continuous damage. Not a debuff, not a slow — real health loss, every second, everywhere in your nation. It is why in Rise of Nations you cannot simply mass an army and walk it into someone's capital, and it is why the game is about the frontier rather than about the base.
SituationDamageNote
Inside enemy borders, unsupplied1.8 HP/sScales with their Civic attrition techs
Inside enemy borders, in supply0Supply is the counter — see below
Inside your own borders−1.0 HP/sRegeneration. Falling back to your own land heals you.
Neutral ground0Most of the early map

The consequence is a genuinely different shape of war from the counter-triangle skirmishing in 04 · Roster: you advance your border first and your army second. Taking ground means taking or razing the city that projects it.

3. Cities

RoN's territory instrument is the city, not the barracks. Cities are capped, expensive, and the main thing you fight over.

Capturing a city flips its territory instantly. Razing it removes the territory entirely and leaves neutral ground. That choice — take it or burn it — is a real decision every time.

5. Nations

RoN ships 18 nations with unique powers and units. That is a large content commitment, so: six nations at first, one per age theme, each with two powers and one unique unit. Six is enough for replay variety and maps onto art we either have or need anyway.

6. The Armageddon counter — and it answers Q1

This resolves the project's oldest open question Q1 asked: is launching a nuke a win button, or a loss condition disguised as one? Rise of Nations already has the right answer and it is better than anything I proposed: a shared Armageddon counter.

Every nuclear detonation, by anyone, adds 1 to a counter visible to all players. When it reaches the threshold, the world ends and every player with nuclear weapons loses — including the one who was winning.

SettingValue
Threshold5 detonations
Who losesEvery nation that has ever launched. A non-nuclear survivor wins.
VisibilityA permanent counter in the HUD from the moment the first silo is built
DecayNone. It only goes up.

This makes the warhead exactly what 01 · Concept wanted it to be: not a button that wins, but a button that spends a shared, finite, irreversible resource — and the fifth press kills everyone. You spend seventy minutes building a civilisation and are handed the means to make all of it irrelevant, including yours. That is the game's argument, and it is now a mechanic rather than a theme.

The ABM site matters more under this rule, because intercepting does not increment the counter. Defence is genuinely better than retaliation, which is the correct lesson to build into a system.

7. Conquer the World — later, not at launch

RoN's campaign is a turn-based map of the world over which you fight real-time battles. It is the best thing in the game and it is a whole second project. Backlogged deliberately, with one constraint honoured now so it stays possible: the 20 Hz simulation in 10 · Tech takes its entire starting state from a seed plus a setup struct, so a meta-layer can hand it "this army, this territory, this age" without any rework.

Implementation note — territory is a grid, and it is cheap

Borders are computed on the same 1 m grid as the flow field (validated in 08 · Map against Supreme Commander 2's shipped convention). One byte per cell holding the owning player index: 410 KB at the largest map size, recomputed only when a city is founded, captured, razed or a border tech completes — which is a handful of times per match, not per frame. Rendering is one fullscreen pass sampling that grid.

Attrition then costs one grid lookup per unit per sim tick — 200 lookups at 20 Hz. Free.
