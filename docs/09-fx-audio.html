09 · Feel

Animation, VFX & Sound

The layer that decides whether the game feels made or generated. Everything here is cheap except the three things that are not, and those three are named.

The doctrine — three speeds, never blended

Carried over from MOTION.md, and it holds here World speed — 4–30 s, linear. Clouds, water, smoke drift, day cycle.
Business speed — 200–600 ms, smooth with a slight overshoot. UI, build placement, selection rings, resource ticks.
Violence speed — 60–180 ms, no easing at all. Impacts, muzzle flash, recoil, death.

The rule is that these never blend into each other. A death that eases is a death that reads as a UI animation. Violence is the absence of easing — that is the whole trick, and it is free.

The count lives in one place The whole-game animation inventory — ~79 authored clips, 14 code-driven motions, 13 rigs, by weapon class and in build order — is in PROGRESS.md §5b, because it is pending work and changes as clips land. This page keeps the doctrine; that section keeps the list.

The sword arc, specifically

It was asked for by name, so it gets specified. The arm is not the effect. A melee swing reads from four things, in this order of importance:

  1. The trail — a 6-segment ribbon along the blade path, 120 ms lifetime, white-hot at the tip fading to nothing. Without it the arm is just moving.
  2. The hitstop — on impact, freeze both units for 60 ms. This is the single highest value-per-line effect in the entire game.
  3. The anticipation — 4 frames of wind-up in the opposite direction before the swing.
  4. The arm. Last. It carries the other three.

This is Age VI's payoff and the most-watched 14 seconds in the game. It is scripted as a sequence, not a particle system, because it must be identical and correct every time.

The two decisions that make it land The delayed boom — sound arriving 2.5 s after the light is physically true and almost nobody does it, and it is the difference between an explosion and an event. The silence — cutting all audio at detonation makes a loud moment feel enormous, where layering more sound would just make it loud. Both are free.

Blood and death persistence

Asked for specifically: bodies and blood stay for 10–15 s. Settled at 12 s, with this lifecycle:

t after deathWhat
0.0 sDeath clip plays. A small directional blood burst — 8 particles, 300 ms, gravity on.
0.4 sA blood decal projects onto the terrain under the body, growing from 0 to full over 600 ms
0 – 12 sBody and decal hold at full. Collision off; other units walk over.
12 – 14 sBody sinks 0.3 m into the ground while fading. Decal fades to 35% opacity.
14 s →Decal remains at 35% until the decal budget evicts it, oldest first.

Budget: 250 decals and 60 corpses on screen. Oldest-first eviction. A big battlefield should stay stained long after the fight — the lingering decal is what makes a map feel fought over — but the corpses must clear or a late-game field becomes unreadable.

Ship a toggle Settings → Blood: On / Reduced / Off. Reduced keeps the decals and drops the particle burst. Some storefronts and some regions require it, and retrofitting a gore toggle late is far more work than adding the branch now.

Death vocals — the honest note

Asked for specifically. 4 variants per faction per age, pitch-randomised ±8%, and cut short — 0.5 to 0.9 s. Three constraints that matter more than the recording:

  • Hard voice limit of 6 simultaneous death sounds, with the nearest to camera winning. Twenty units dying at once without a limiter is a wall of noise that sounds comic rather than grim.
  • A 120 ms minimum gap between any two. Without it a volley kill sounds like one distorted sound.
  • Distance-gated. Beyond 45 m, deaths get a muffled thud instead of a voice. Grim up close, abstract at a distance — which is also the game's argument.

The 35-second showcase

Asked for: “how the map will look for 35 seconds”. This is a scripted camera flythrough, rendered from the real Unity scene at 4K60, and it doubles as the regression test for the whole art pipeline — if it renders correctly, the pipeline is intact.

Why this specific sequence It is not a trailer, it is a checklist rendered as a camera move. Every shot is a system that can silently break: water, scatter, animation, terrain baking, the age swap, the silo state machine. Run it after every art change and look at it. Fifteen seconds of watching beats an hour of debugging a build.
