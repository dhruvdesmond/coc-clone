Unity Architecture

How the game is actually built. The decisions here are the expensive-to-reverse ones, so each states its reasoning and its escape hatch.

Decision 1 — MonoBehaviour, not DOTS. For now.

The benchmarks are not in dispute: ECS delivers 5–20× over MonoBehaviour at thousands of entities, and one commonly cited comparison had MonoBehaviour at 7–8 FPS on 100,000 entities where DOTS held 60. DOTS is the right shape for RTS crowds.

We are still not starting there, for one reason: our unit cap is 200, not 100,000. The honest assessment of DOTS in 2026 is that it is production-ready for experienced teams but still has a steep learning curve, a worse developer experience, and immature debugging — and this project's scarcest resource is not CPU, it is the time of two people learning Blender and Unity at once.

WhatCountMonoBehaviour verdict
Units200 capTrivial
Buildings~150Trivial
Ground cover instances~18,000Not GameObjects. GPU-instanced, no components. See below.
Projectiles peak~300Pooled, updated from one manager, no per-object Update()
The escape hatch, designed in from day one Simulation and presentation are separated. Simulation is plain C# classes in a Sim assembly with no UnityEngine dependency beyond math types — units are structs in arrays, ticked at a fixed 20 Hz. Presentation is MonoBehaviours that read the sim and interpolate. If we ever need DOTS, we port one assembly and the rest of the game does not notice. If we never need it, we have lost nothing and gained a simulation that is testable headless.

Decision 2 — Fixed-tick simulation at 20 Hz

Sim.Tick()   every 50 ms, deterministic, integer/fixed-point where it matters
Render       every frame, interpolating between the last two sim states
  • Testable. The sim runs headless with no Unity at all, so the economy can be swept by bots the way the Merchants of War economy was — thousands of runs, before a single balance argument.
  • Cheap. AI, pathfinding and combat run 20× a second, not 60–240.
  • Replay and save become trivial — a seed plus the order log reproduces a whole match.
  • Multiplayer stays possible later via lockstep, without a rewrite. Not a launch feature.

Two rules that have already been broken once each Anything importing UnityEditor lives in Assets/Editor/ — mixing it into runtime breaks player builds with an error that does not name the cause.
Scenes are generated. The build always regenerates before building. A hand-edit to a generated scene is destroyed on the next build, silently, and the symptom is “my change did nothing”.

The lesson from the mobile build, restated for desktop Triangles were never the problem. Draw calls were. A hall is 35 k triangles and that is fine; it is 252 separate objects and that is not. Join each asset to one mesh per material at export. A building should arrive in Unity as 3–6 renderers, not 252.

Anti-aliasing and graphics settings

Verified — and it overturns the intuitive choice MSAA is the most expensive AA option in URP on desktop GPUs. It is only cheaper on tiled mobile GPUs with no post-processing. It cannot be combined with TAA. And it antialiases geometric edges only — so it will not fix shimmer on alpha-tested grass or fence cards unless alpha-to-coverage is used.

This matters because a game full of thin geometry looks like the textbook case for MSAA, and it is not. Start from SMAA or TAA, not MSAA, and if grass shimmers, reach for alpha-to-coverage before reaching for MSAA.

Two related beliefs failed verification and must not be built on: that MSAA and a post-process AA can be combined (refuted 0–3), and that MSAA is the right pick for thin geometry (refuted 1–2).

Unknown — and it gates the art budget Unity publishes no millisecond figures for URP quality settings, and nothing external validated the budget in the table above. The 16.6 ms / 1,500 draw call / 6 M triangle targets are our assumptions, not measured facts.

Build a synthetic stress scene before committing the art budget: a dressed 420 m map, 18,000 instanced ground-cover meshes, 200 units, measured at 1440p on the target GPU, broken down by AA mode (off / SMAA / TAA / 2× MSAA), shadow distance, and APV on/off.

Save format

A save is the seed, the map archetype, and the order log — not a memory dump. Small, diffable, version-tolerant, and it gives replays for free. A periodic full state snapshot every 2 minutes bounds the reload time.

Slice plan — the order things get built

SliceProvesDone when
0 · BridgeThe pipeline works end to endOne Blender asset arrives in Unity correctly coloured and correctly scaled, via palette.json
1 · One citizenThe core loop's first verbA citizen walks to a tree on a real heightfield, chops, carries, drops off, and the wood counter moves
2 · Build and growThe loop closesPlace a hut, pop cap rises, Town Centre trains citizen #2, Age I economy runs unattended for 10 minutes without breaking
3 · FightCombat and movement at scaleTwo squads of 20 flow-field their way to each other, fight with the counter triangle, leave bodies and blood
4 · AdvanceThe pleasure spikeKnowledge accrues, Feudal researches, every building swaps model in place
5 · OpponentThere is a gameThe utility commander plays a full Age I–II match without idling citizens or trickling attacks
Slice 0 is not a formality On both previous Unity projects the pipeline is where the days went — grey materials, 3 mm meshes, silently empty FBX files. Do slice 0 completely, with one asset, before building a second one. Every asset made before the bridge is proven is an asset that may have to be redone.
